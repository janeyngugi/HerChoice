# HerChoice Proposal

## Introduction

This project focuses on the design and development of a system that is aimed at assisting victims of sexual abuse. This is by providing access to hospitals, therapy institutions, shelters, and educational resources. The system allows victims to report anonymously or pseudo anonymously, the reports can be used in studies in schools, the project emphasizes user safety, confidentiality, and accessibility, ensuring that victims can seek help without fear of being exposed. By combining technology and support, this system demonstrates how digital tools can contribute to addressing sensitive social issues and improving access to critical services.

## Chapter One: Introduction

### Background of study

For quite a long time violence against women has been a serious yet ignored and tolerated problem worldwide. About 1 in every 3 women worldwide has experienced physical and/or sexual violence and mostly by an intimate partner according to World Health Organization statistics. (Organization, 2018). Violence against women(VAW) is usually influenced by inequality, religion, harmful cultural norms, poverty and crises like conflict. Kenya has laws such as sexual Offences Act (2006) and Protection against Domestic Violence Act (2015) but enforcement remains inconsistent. Survivors often face stigma, long legal processes and limited access to Justice. Just as the digital world evolves, this violence against women also evolve. There is now digital abuse like online harassing and cyberbullying. Perpetrators now get to hide behind screens, plot and kill women. (Vasanthi, 2024) Traditional methods of data collection such as surveys and police reports fail to grasp the full scope and severity of this problem. Data has now become a key factor in influencing change. Big data defined by its volume, veracity, value, velocity and variety offers big opportunity to explore complex issues and predict trends. (S, 2025)
This project explores how big data analytics can be harnessed to protect women from violence and survivors to critical support services. By analyzing patterns in social media, emergency calls, health records, and other digital factors it becomes easier to identify risks, predict hotspots of violence and deploy resources effectively. Furthermore, digital platform can be used to create safe, anonymous and accessible channels for survivors to seek help, share stories and find communities. (Nair T, 2023)
The intersection of Technology and social injustice helps us reimagine how we handle and respond to VAW. This proposal outlines a research project that aims to develop a data driven framework for enhancing the safety of women and improving the support for survivors

### Statement of the problem

Sexual abuse is an underreported problem worldwide. This is because there is not a lot of locally available applications or frameworks where victims can report sexual abuse anonymously or pseudo anonymously. Victims have to go to police stations to report the ordeal. despite not all law enforcements officers, some victims may face stigma from dealing with people who are not well equipped to handle the victims. They may also lack real time information to help due to traditional methods of collecting data like presenting forms in police stations that do not explain the severity of the issues. They also have to await long back and forth court processes that are emotionally draining. Victims need a lot of guidance and help after Sexual abuse. They do not need to go to different places around the country to look for therapists and shelters if they are running from abusers to gain all the help they deserve. Previous studies and systems have been developed or explored for different types of Violence against women like Report It Stop it in Africa where victims of Sexual abuse can map their experiencers in Public places. The different studies are broad or do not integrate the reporting, guidance and counselling and suggesting available shelters around Kenya for victims. This project aims at creating a one stop safe place for victims where they can be able to access all the help they need. Making it easier for victims of sexual abuse. Despite the growing digital data, few works have been made to ensure that this information is used to help end VAW. There is a need for change so that we can leverage big data to detect patterns of abuse, predict risks and connect survivors to timely and appropriate help.

## Chapter 3: Methodology

### System requirements specification

#### Functional

1. The system shall allow users to log in or view as guest.
2. User is asked to select whether they want to report or just want to learn of other survivor’s stories.
3. The system once clicked on report will ask whether they want to report anonymously or pseudo anonymously and ask for their location.
4. The system shall give a report questionnaire to collect data for both and provide a hotline to police station in case they also want to take official channel.
5. Depending on severity of the abuse case, the system will direct the victims to different 24/7 hospitals. therapy counselling and shelters around where they are.
6. The system shall provide approved stories to share anonymously by victims.  

#### Non-functional

1. The system shall run efficiently on devices with low processing power.
2. A simple UI suitable for both computer literate and new to computers.
3. Secure the data collected against hacking.

### System design

#### Business model supported

Supporting safe and helpful environment by providing anonymous questionnaires, stories from surviving victims and reassuring pop up messages to remind them it’s not their fault. The model encourages victim to report at their own time, while also allowing them to see that getting out of that situation is at the tip of their fingers they have got everything they need.

#### User interface design

a. A home screen with buttons such as Report, Survivor stories and Call Now Helplines.
b. Reassurance text screens pop ups.
c. Screens with different therapist contacts, locations and instant feedback.
d. A screen showing education and support like FAQs about therapy and shelters.
The interface will use simple colors, simple icons and empathetic, non-triggering text.

#### Data types implemented

1. Float type for latitudes and longitudes location data.
2. String type for user input data and helpline resource data.
3. JSON or simple text files for local storage of reports

#### Algorithms used

1. Location and mapping Algorithm: Selects nearest hospitals, therapy centers and shelters depending on victim’s location.
2. Data analysis and Pattern Algorithm: Categories reports based on type of urgency or urgency.
3. Security and privacy Algorithm: Role based access to reports based on roles to ensure safety of victims.
4. Data Saving Algorithm: Stores victims reports in local storage.

#### System architecture

Modular structure consists of:

1. User Interface Module
2. Report questionnaire Module
3. Helplines Module
4. Data Storage Module
5. Education and survivor stories Module
